What Cenara is

Cenara identifies ingredients from a photo and writes recipes built around them. You take a picture of your fridge, your counter, or the shopping you just brought in; Cenara tells you what it sees, you correct anything it got wrong, and it writes recipes for what you actually have.

The recipes are generated, and that matters

Every recipe in Cenara is written by an AI model, for your specific ingredients, at the moment you ask for it. Nobody has cooked it. Nobody has tested it. It has not been reviewed by a chef, a nutritionist, or anyone at all.

This has consequences you need to take seriously:

  • Use your own judgement on food safety. Cooking times and temperatures are suggestions, not verified instructions. Cook meat, poultry, fish, and eggs to safe internal temperatures regardless of what a recipe says.
  • Allergies are your responsibility. Cenara lets you record allergies and tries hard to respect them, including checks on the server that discard recipes contradicting what you have told it. It can still be wrong. An ingredient can be misidentified from a photo, a product can contain an allergen that is not in its name, and a model can make a mistake. If a mistake would be dangerous for you, read the packaging yourself, every time.
  • Dietary labels can be wrong for the same reasons.
  • Ingredient recognition is imperfect. Review what Cenara found before cooking from it. That is what the review screen is for.

Cenara is a cooking assistant, not a food safety authority or a source of medical or nutritional advice. If you have a medical condition affected by what you eat, talk to a professional, not an app.
