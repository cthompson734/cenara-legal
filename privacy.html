Cenara — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 11 September 2026

The short version

Cenara sends the photo you take to a server so an AI model can identify the ingredients in it. The photo is used for that one request and is never stored. Your ingredients, recipes, and shopping list live on your phone. There is no advertising, no tracking, and no analytics company involved.

What leaves your device

Photos you scan. When you scan ingredients, the image is sent over HTTPS to Cenara's server, which passes it to Anthropic's Claude API to identify what is in it. The image is held in memory for the length of that request and is not written to disk, not placed in a database, and not retained after the response. Cenara's server records only the size of the image in bytes and the number of ingredients found.

Ingredient names, when you ask for recipes. The ingredients you confirmed, along with the preferences you have set — dietary requirements, allergies, serving size, units, maximum cooking time, and your spice rack — are sent so the model can write recipes that respect them. These are not stored on the server either.

An anonymous install identifier. On first launch Cenara generates a random identifier and keeps it in your device's Keychain. It is sent with each request so the server can apply usage limits fairly across users. It is not derived from your device, is not an advertising identifier, is not linked to your name, Apple ID, or email, and is meaningless to anyone but Cenara's own server.

What stays on your device

Your ingredient inventory, spice rack, generated and saved recipes, shopping list, scan history, and preferences are stored locally on your iPhone and are not transmitted anywhere. Deleting the app deletes them. Settings → Delete my data removes them without deleting the app.

Who else processes your data

Anthropic receives your scan photos and ingredient lists in order to run the models that identify ingredients and write recipes. Anthropic's API terms state that inputs and outputs from commercial API traffic are not used to train their models. See https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms.

Google Cloud hosts Cenara's server (Cloud Run, US region). Requests pass through their infrastructure in transit.

Apple handles subscription billing entirely. Cenara never sees your card details, billing address, or Apple ID.

There is no advertising network, no analytics SDK, and no data broker. Cenara does not track you across apps or websites and does not ask for permission to, because it has nothing to do with the answer.

Permissions the app asks for

  • Camera — to photograph ingredients. Images are used only for scanning.
  • Photo library — only when you choose an existing photo to scan. Cenara reads the one image you pick and does not browse your library.

Children

Cenara is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from them.

Your choices

  • Delete everything: Settings → Delete my data.
  • Stop all transmission: stop scanning, or delete the app.
  • Manage or cancel your subscription: Settings → Manage subscription, which opens Apple's own subscription management.

Security, honestly stated

Traffic between the app and the server is encrypted with HTTPS. The server is authenticated and rate limited. No system is perfectly secure, and Cenara is a small independent app rather than an organisation with a security team — weigh that when deciding what to photograph.

Changes

If the data flow changes — particularly if Cenara ever begins storing photos or your corrections to improve the app — this policy will be updated before that change ships, and the change will be described here rather than buried.
